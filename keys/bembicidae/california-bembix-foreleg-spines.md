# California Bembix female foreleg-spine comparison and short key

Last updated: 2026-07-31

Repository note: this is now a genus-level `Bembix` note inside the broader
Bembicidae workspace. For the family-level eastern-Sierra scope, use
`checklists/bembicidae/eastern-sierra-working-list.md`.

## Scope and main takeaway

This note compares California `Bembix` females for a specimen with
conspicuously spiny forelegs, with emphasis on coastal Santa Barbara County and
the Dangermond Preserve.

The literature reviewed here rarely treats female fore-tibial spine length
itself as diagnostic. The repeated diagnostic character is the rake on the
female fore basitarsus: the number and development of long rake setae on the
first tarsal segment of the foreleg. Some `Bembix americana` subspecies also
show black maculation on the fore tibia and midtibia, but that is a color
character, not spine length.

For a mainland coastal Santa Barbara County or Dangermond Preserve female with
conspicuously spiny forelegs, `Bembix americana comata` is the strongest
candidate in the current literature. For a northern Channel Islands female,
especially from Santa Cruz, Santa Rosa, or San Miguel islands,
`Bembix americana hamata` is the stronger candidate.

## Taxonomic update search

Before finalizing this comparison, a post-1971 `Bembix` search was run against
Pulawski's current `Bembix` catalog and targeted web/literature searches. The
search found:

- `Bembix inyoensis` Kimsey and Kimsey, 1981: valid California species from
  Death Valley National Monument, Inyo County. Add to the California checklist,
  but do not use for a coastal Santa Barbara/Dangermond identification without
  new locality evidence.
- `Bembix americana dugi` Menke, 1985: valid California subspecies from San
  Clemente Island. Add under `B. americana`, but it is not a mainland Santa
  Barbara/Dangermond candidate.
- `Bembix boharti` Griswold, 1983: nearby Baja California species with seven
  female fore-basitarsal rake setae. Exclude from the California checklist
  unless new California records are found.

No other post-1971 California `Bembix` species were found in the sources
searched on 2026-07-31.

## Short key for a California female with conspicuously spiny forelegs

This is a reduced key for the long-foreleg-spine question. It does not replace
Bohart and Horning's full California key.

1. Forewing with a brownish cloud over the basal three-fourths, cloud more
   prominent in females. -> `Bembix melanaspis`

1'. Forewing clear or nearly clear, without that brownish cloud. -> 2

2. Female last visible tergite/pygidial area longitudinally rugose. -> 3

2'. Female last visible tergite/pygidial area not longitudinally rugose. -> 5

3. Labrum longer than eye height; second submarginal cell acutely prolonged
   basad; fore-basitarsal rake with seven or eight setae. -> `Bembix rugosa`

3'. Labrum shorter than eye height, or scutum with a prominent U-shaped yellow
   mark, or female unknown. -> 4

4. Prominent U-shaped yellow mark on scutum; labrum dentate; fore basitarsus
   with six or seven setae. -> `Bembix dentilabris`

4'. Female unknown; species known only from eastern Inyo County males in Bohart
    and Horning. -> `Bembix frommeri` only if later female material and locality
    support it.

5. Scape considerably shorter than median length of clypeus. -> 6

5'. Scape nearly as long as, or as long as, median length of clypeus. -> 8

6. Labrum dentate or humped near middle in profile. -> 7

6'. Labrum not dentate; second submarginal cell nearly right-angled; fore
    basitarsus with six to seven rake setae. -> `Bembix stenebdoma`

7. Second submarginal cell acutely prolonged basad; scutum black except
   laterally; small desert species. -> `Bembix gillaspyi`

7'. Second submarginal cell nearly right-angled; prominent U-shaped scutal mark.
    -> `Bembix dentilabris`

8. Mandible slender and nearly straight, with very small subapical denticle. -> 9

8'. Mandible moderately stout and curved, with one or two well-developed
    subapical teeth. -> 10

9. Pale tergal bands broad, whitish or very pale yellow, and not enclosing black
   spots; very rare northern California taxon. -> `Bembix pallidipicta`

9'. Tergal markings yellow when extensive and often enclosing black spots. ->
    `Bembix occidentalis`

10. Middle tibial spur at least half as long as basitarsus; female scutum dark;
    fore-basitarsal rake usually with six setae. -> `Bembix amoena`

10'. Middle tibial spur shorter than half basitarsus, or `americana` group
     characters present. -> 11

11. Middle tibial spur shorter and slender, less than half as long as
    basitarsus; females often with discal yellow scutal markings and enclosed
    black spots on terga; California records from Inyo and San Bernardino in
    Bohart and Horning. -> `Bembix sayi`

11'. Labrum all pale or partly black; abdomen extensively maculate or island
     melanistic; no more than seven fully developed setae in the front
     basitarsal rake; `B. americana` complex. -> 12

12. Mainland coastal Santa Barbara County or Dangermond Preserve specimen;
    margin between clypeus and frons nearly always pale; midtibia not
    black-marked; female front basitarsal comb usually with six well-developed
    setae, sometimes with a weak basal seventh. -> `Bembix americana comata`

12'. Northern Channel Islands specimen from Santa Cruz, Santa Rosa, or San
     Miguel; margin between clypeus and frons with black line; midtibia often
     with black streak or spots; female front basitarsal comb usually with
     seven well-developed setae. -> `Bembix americana hamata`

12''. San Nicolas Island specimen; face and abdomen strongly melanistic, labrum
      mostly black, tergite VI all black. -> `Bembix americana nicolai`

12'''. San Clemente Island specimen; consult Rust, Menke, and Miller (1985) for
       diagnosis. -> `Bembix americana dugi`

## Practical interpretation for the specimen

If the specimen is from the Dangermond Preserve or mainland coastal Santa
Barbara County, the literature currently points first to `Bembix americana
comata`. Bohart and Horning document mainland Santa Barbara County localities
and specifically include a Goleta female sample with a conspicuous
fore-basitarsal rake, averaging just over six rake setae, and frequent black
maculation on the foretibia.

If the specimen is from Santa Cruz, Santa Rosa, or San Miguel islands, the
long-spined impression may fit `Bembix americana hamata` better because females
usually have seven well-developed fore-basitarsal rake setae and heavier tibial
black maculation.

If the specimen is from an interior desert locality, compare `B. rugosa`,
`B. dentilabris`, `B. stenebdoma`, `B. gillaspyi`, and `B. sayi` using the full
Bohart and Horning key. Do not assign `B. boharti` to California without a
California record.
