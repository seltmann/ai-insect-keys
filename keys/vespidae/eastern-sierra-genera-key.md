# Eastern Sierra Vespidae Genus Key

Last updated: 2026-08-26

## Scope

This is a working genus-level key for Vespidae likely in the Eastern Sierra,
treated here as a strict Inyo plus Mono County core, with a short adjacent
Sierra/east-slope watchlist where useful.

Use this as a specimen key, not a field-photo shortcut. Several couplets require
wing venation, tibial spurs, tarsal claws, the clypeus, the pronotum,
metasomal tergum 1, the base of tergum 2, or sex-specific characters.

Taxonomic note: `Dolichovespula` is treated here as a genus, following modern
usage. Bohart and Bechtel (1957) treated it as `Vespula (Dolichovespula)`.
`Polistes` is used broadly for the practical paper-wasp route; some data sources
split or route part of this fauna differently. A few GBIF/BOLD records are
assigned to `Rhynchalastor`; those are treated here as unresolved
`Stenodynerus`-complex signals until checked by specimens.

## Species-Level Working Count

Using the same strict Eastern Sierra scope as this key, Inyo plus Mono County,
current GBIF species-level records across the three relevant backbone family
buckets (`Vespidae`, `Eumenidae`, and `Masaridae`) give 32 species-level names
in 12 genera. County counts are 23 species-level names for Inyo and 22 for Mono;
the strict Inyo plus Mono union is 32 because many species occur in both
counties.

The Richards (1966) `Pseudomasaris` reference supplied by the user raises the
literature-adjusted working count to at least 34 Vespidae species for the strict
area. That reference adds `Pseudomasaris macswaini` from Inyo County and
`Pseudomasaris micheneri` from the Inyo Mountains, and it independently confirms
high-elevation `Pseudomasaris wheeleri` records from Mono and Inyo counties.

For `Pseudomasaris` specifically, the strict working list is now 9 species:
`Pseudomasaris basirufus`, `Pseudomasaris coquilletti`,
`Pseudomasaris edwardsii` (spelled `edwardsi` in the current GBIF backbone),
`Pseudomasaris maculifrons`, `Pseudomasaris macswaini`,
`Pseudomasaris micheneri`, `Pseudomasaris vespoides`,
`Pseudomasaris wheeleri`, and `Pseudomasaris zonalis`.

## Subfamily Key

1a. Apical flagellomeres, from about flagellomere III-V onward, consolidated
into a club, sometimes flattened; fore wing with two submarginal cells; first
subdiscoidal cell in contact with the medial cell; pollen wasp.
`Masarinae` - go to the Masarinae route.

1b. Apical flagellomeres not forming a club, though male antenna may be hooked
or coiled; fore wing usually with three submarginal cells.
Go to 2.

2a. Small, compact arid-region wasp with unique euparagiine venation; if the
specimen appears to be `Euparagia`, this subfamily has only one extant genus.
`Euparagiinae` - go to the Euparagiinae route.

2b. Not as above.
Go to 3.

3a. Mid tibia with one spur; gena tapering nearly to a point ventrally;
posterobasal corner of mandible close to the eye margin; tarsal claws cleft;
solitary mason or potter wasp.
`Eumeninae` - go to the Eumeninae route.

3b. Mid tibia with two spurs; gena broad ventrally; posterobasal corner of
mandible separated from the ventral eye margin by more than the diameter of the
antennal socket; tarsal claws simple; social or socially parasitic.
Go to 4.

4a. Clypeus pointed apically, at most minutely truncate in the female; head and
body with very short, inconspicuous hair; metasomal tergum 1 almost straight or
gently arched in profile; nests open-faced and without an envelope.
`Polistinae` - go to the Polistinae route.

4b. Clypeus broadly truncate or emarginate apically; head and body with longer
hair; metasomal tergum 1 with an abrupt anterior declivity; nests enclosed in a
paper envelope.
`Vespinae` - go to the Vespinae route.

## Euparagiinae Route

Eupa1a. Specimen is a euparagiine. The only extant genus is `Euparagia`.

## Masarinae Route

Masa1a. Specimen is a masarine from the strict working fauna.
`Pseudomasaris`

Species-level aid only: Richards (1966) separates males using antennal club and
leg characters. Local examples include `P. vespoides`, with the antennal club
concave beneath and longer than antennal segment 3; `P. edwardsii`, with
antennal segments 6-7 wider than the short club, which is concave beneath; and
`P. basirufus`, with the penultimate antennal segments and club bare beneath.
Use the full Richards key for species-level determinations.

Female `Pseudomasaris edwardsii` checkpoint, after Richards (1966): no sharp
transverse ridge just above the antennal sockets; clypeus shagreened or
punctate rather than strongly transversely rugose; fore basitarsus clearly
shorter than the mid basitarsus; mid femur flattened beneath with a more or less
distinct anteroventral ridge; mid tibia almost always with a distinct anterior
prominence near the middle; humeri with an obtuse anterior ridge; yellow of the
upper orbits not produced toward the ocelli; propodeal spines longer than in
`P. zonalis`; yellow markings more extensive; metapleuron usually with a small
yellow spot; size intermediate among the similar black-and-yellow species.

## Polistinae Route

P1a. Metasoma sessile; first metasomal segment at most slightly longer than
wide; tarsomere 4 of the mid and hind legs symmetrical.
`Polistes`

P1b. Metasoma petiolate; first metasomal segment more than twice as long as
wide; tarsomere 4 of the mid and hind legs strongly asymmetrical.
`Mischocyttarus` - adjacent/watchlist for this draft.

## Vespinae Route

V1a. Very large vespine, fore wing about 16-24 mm; ferruginous markings on head
and mesosoma; posterior ocelli separated from hind margin of vertex by several
times an ocellar diameter; pronotal carina nearly complete.
`Vespa` - outside the current Eastern Sierra working fauna.

V1b. Smaller vespine, fore wing usually about 8-18 mm; head and mesosoma
without ferruginous markings; posterior ocelli separated from hind margin of
vertex by at most about one ocellar diameter; pronotal carina absent or broadly
interrupted medially.
Go to V2.

V2a. Malar or oculomalar space long, about as long as or longer than the
penultimate antennal segment, or about 2x the mid ocellar diameter; pronotal
carina present or vertical pronotal carina well defined.
`Dolichovespula`

V2b. Malar or oculomalar space short, at most about half the length of the
penultimate antennal segment, or at most one mid ocellar diameter; pronotal
carina absent or only faint below.
`Vespula`

## Eumeninae Route

Eu1a. Metasoma distinctly petiolate; segment 1 at least twice as long as wide
and only about half as wide as segment 2.
Go to Eu2.

Eu1b. Metasoma not petiolate; segment 1 at most about 1.4x as long as wide and
at least about 0.7x as wide as segment 2.
Go to Eu3.

Eu2a. Clypeal margin strongly produced and angulate, extending distinctly below
the level of the lower eye margin; minimum width of metasomal segment 2 only
slightly less than width of segment 1; fore wing vein M nearly straight at the
base of submarginal cell II.
`Eumenes`

Eu2b. Clypeal margin truncate and more or less straight, not extending below the
lower eye margin; minimum width of metasomal segment 2 less than half width of
segment 1; fore wing vein M with two distinct kinks at the base of submarginal
cell II.
`Zethus` - outside the current Eastern Sierra working fauna.

Eu3a. `Pterocheilus` route. Tegula evenly rounded posteriorly, not emarginate
adjoining the parategula and not reaching its apex; mesepisternum without an
epicnemial carina; pronotal transverse carina usually present and well developed
dorsally. Female with 3-segmented labial palpus broadly flattened and strongly
fringed with hairs, forming a psammophore. Male antenna with 12 or 13 articles;
apical flagellomere modified or very small; flagellomere I about as long as the
scape.
`Pterocheilus`

Eu3b. Not as above; female labial palpi not developed into a psammophore, and
male antenna/pronotum/tegula combination not matching `Pterocheilus`.
Go to Eu4.

Eu4a. Anterior face of pronotum with a pair of medial pits.
Go to Eu5.

Eu4b. Anterior face of pronotum lacking a pair of medial pits.
Go to Eu6.

Eu5a. Metasomal tergum 1 with a long overhanging hind margin covering an
acarinarium, a chamber for symbiotic mites; base of tergum 2 essentially smooth,
sometimes minutely roughened but not pitted.
`Parancistrocerus`

Eu5b. Metasomal tergum 1 with a short overhang; space below not serving as an
acarinarium; base of tergum 2 usually with a row of shallow to deep pits,
sometimes obsolete medially.
`Stenodynerus` sensu the atlas. Some western database records may need checking
against `Rhynchalastor`.

Eu6a. Metasomal tergum 1 with a transverse carina at the summit, or pronotum
lacking a transverse carina.
Go to Eu7.

Eu6b. Pronotum with transverse carina, and metasomal tergum 1 without a
transverse carina.
Go to Eu9.

Eu7a. Metasomal tergum 1 with a shallow median groove posterior to the carina;
male antenna simple apically; omaulus well defined in both sexes and curved
backward ventrally.
`Symmorphus`

Eu7b. Metasomal tergum 1 without a median groove; male antenna hooked or coiled
apically; female without omaulus.
Go to Eu8.

Eu8a. Tegula rounded posteriorly; tergum 1 lacking transverse carina; male with
apical six flagellomeres flattened and coiled and mid femur with two deep
ventral emarginations; female cephalic foveae as far apart as the hind ocelli.
`Odynerus`

Eu8b. Tegula pointed posteriorly; tergum 1 with transverse carina, rarely weak;
male apical flagellomeres neither flattened nor coiled, with flagellomere XI
reflexed; female cephalic foveae closer together than the hind ocelli.
`Ancistrocerus`

Species-level aid: [Eastern Sierra Ancistrocerus Species Key](eastern-sierra-ancistrocerus-species-key.md).

Eu9a. Flagellomere I approximately as long as II; fore wing vein M with two
distinct kinks at the base of submarginal cell II; propodeal valvula large, with
free upper, lower, and posterior margins; small wasps, usually less than 9 mm.
`Leptochilus` - outside the current strict working fauna, but check if the
geographic scope expands.

Eu9b. Flagellomere I at least about 1.3x as long as II; fore wing vein M nearly
straight or only slightly angled at the base of submarginal cell II; propodeal
valvula dorsally fused to submarginal carina; usually larger than 9 mm.
Go to Eu10.

Eu10a. Metasomal tergum 1 only about 0.70-0.75x as wide as segment 2; terga 2
and 3 with thin, translucent, reflexed apical lamellae; propodeum rounded
laterally.
`Parazumia` - outside the current strict working fauna.

Eu10b. Width of metasomal tergum 1 subequal to segment 2; terga 2 and 3 usually
without the above translucent reflexed lamellae, or if lamellae occur then the
propodeum is angled posterolaterally.
Go to Eu11.

Eu11a. Mandible entirely black; postocular spot absent; body black with ivory
markings; only tergum 1 with apical fascia; tegula with truncate hind margin
nearly perpendicular to the main body axis.
`Monobia` - outside the current strict working fauna.

Eu11b. Mandible with at least a small pale spot near the base, sometimes
extensively ferruginous; postocular spot pale to ferruginous; body variably
marked, often yellow or ivory; apical fasciae at least on terga 1 and 2.
`Euodynerus`

## Practical Checkpoints

| First character to check | Most useful split |
| --- | --- |
| Apical antennal club plus two submarginal cells | `Pseudomasaris` and Masarinae |
| Very small arid `Euparagia`-like wasp | `Euparagia` and Euparagiinae |
| Mid tibial spur count and tarsal claws | Eumeninae versus social wasps |
| Pointed versus truncate/emarginate clypeus | Polistinae versus Vespinae |
| Long versus short malar/oculomalar space | `Dolichovespula` versus `Vespula` |
| Petiolate metasoma | `Eumenes` versus non-petiolate Eumeninae |
| Pronotal pits plus tergum 2 base | `Parancistrocerus` versus `Stenodynerus` |
| Female psammophore | `Pterocheilus` |
