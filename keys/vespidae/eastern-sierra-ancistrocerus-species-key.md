# Eastern Sierra Ancistrocerus Species Key

Last updated: 2026-08-26

## Scope

This is a working species-level key for `Ancistrocerus` from the same strict
Eastern Sierra scope used by the genus key: Inyo plus Mono County, California.
It is intended for pinned adult specimens, especially the current male-heavy
material.

Use genus-level characters first. This page assumes the specimen has already
run to `Ancistrocerus`: tegula pointed posteriorly, metasomal tergum 1 usually
with a transverse carina, male apical flagellomeres neither flattened nor
coiled, and male flagellomere XI reflexed.

Color is useful as supporting evidence, but do not lead with color alone. The
important checkpoints here are the notauli and metanotum, the profile and
pubescence of sternum 2, the parategula and humeral angle, and sex-specific
clypeal or marking characters.

## Quick Male-Heavy Route

1a. Mesoscutum with notauli strongly impressed and obvious, dividing the scutum
into a median lobe and lateral lobes; metanotum dark or black. Propodeal
concavity and lower latero-ventral propodeal area smooth, shining, and nearly
impunctate if visible. Male sternum 7 broad apically and antennal hook short.
`Ancistrocerus capra`

1b. Notauli not strongly impressed in that way, or the metanotum/propodeal
combination does not fit.
Go to 2.

2a. Sternum 2, in lateral view just behind the basal transverse groove,
strongly truncate or almost vertically sloping; base not with a deep median
longitudinal furrow. Body relatively short and stubby. Parategula narrow;
humeral angle obtuse and humeri not projecting; propodeum with dorsal carinae.
Male clypeus emarginate apically and about as wide as long.
`Ancistrocerus bustamente`

2b. Sternum 2 behind the basal transverse groove evenly convex or only gently
swollen, not abruptly truncate/vertical. Mesosoma relatively elongate and
slender; in females, posterior scutum and scutellum conspicuously flattened in
one plane.
Go to 3.

3a. Male: sternum 2 behind the basal groove evenly covered with appressed
micropubescence; middle flagellomeres usually black or dark beneath; usually
only terga 1-4 with apical fasciae; metasomal markings usually ivory, rarely
yellow. Female: markings ivory to pale yellow; clypeus usually black,
sometimes with a pair of pale ventral spots; sternum 4 and tergum 5 without
pale markings; scutellum usually black, rarely with a pair of small pale spots.
`Ancistrocerus albolacteus`

3b. Male: sternum 2 without appressed micropubescence medially; flagellum
yellow to orange beneath; at least terga 1-5 with apical fasciae; metasomal
markings usually bright yellow, rarely pale yellow. Female: markings bright
yellow; clypeus at least with a pair of dorsal spots, often confluent with
ventral spots; sternum 4 with yellow markings; tergum 5 almost always with a
yellow apical fascia; scutellum with a pair of yellow spots.
`Ancistrocerus adiabatus`

## Character Notes

Sternum 2 is the underside of the second metasomal segment. For this key, view
it from the side and from slightly below. First decide whether the base is
abruptly vertical/truncate behind the basal groove (`bustamente`) or evenly
convex/not abrupt (`adiabatus`/`albolacteus`). Then, for males in the latter
pair, use low-angle light to check whether appressed micropubescence covers the
median area behind the groove.

Notauli are the paired grooves on the mesoscutum. In `capra`, they should be
strong and obvious enough to make the scutum look divided into a middle lobe
and lateral lobes. Pair that with a dark metanotum before calling `capra`.

The `adiabatus` and `albolacteus` split is the most delicate endpoint in this
regional key. Males should be checked on sternum-2 pubescence before relying on
marking extent. Females are separated more by the extent and color of pale
markings, especially clypeus, scutellum, sternum 4, and tergum 5.

If a specimen hits the strong-notauli path but has dull, shagreened, punctate,
or striolate propodeal surfaces instead of smooth shining areas, pause rather
than forcing it to `capra`; that combination runs toward taxa outside this
strict four-endpoint working key in Bequaert's broader key.

## Occurrence And Name Caveats

The species list was screened from GBIF `Ancistrocerus` occurrences in
California and then filtered to Inyo and Mono County by GADM county names on
2026-08-26. The full paged pull returned 12 strict-area records: species-level
records for `A. adiabatus`, `A. albolacteus`, `A. bustamente`, and two records
displayed by GBIF as `A. capensis`; genus-only and BOLD-only records were kept
as unresolved.

The two GBIF `A. capensis` records are not used as `capensis` endpoints here.
Their source iNaturalist observations are currently identified as
`Ancistrocerus capra`, research grade, and were last updated under that name
after the 2023 `capra` split. Treat `capensis` in the local GBIF result as a
stale or interpreted name to be checked at the source.
