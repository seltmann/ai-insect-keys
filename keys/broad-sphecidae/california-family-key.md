# California broad-Sphecidae family key and field-screen notes

Last updated: 2026-08-27

## Scope

This is a working key for California spheciform wasps in the broad
`Sphecidae` sense used by Bohart and Menke (1976): Apoidea excluding bees. It
is intended for repository work on Dangermond Preserve and Eastern Sierra
specimens, not as a replacement for genus-level revisions.

The key preserves older Bohart and Menke characters but routes specimens to the
current family buckets used in the shared working notes:

- Ammoplanidae
- Ampulicidae
- Astatidae
- Bembicidae
- Crabronidae
- Mellinidae
- Pemphredonidae
- Philanthidae
- Psenidae
- Sphecidae, restricted modern sense

Because the family limits of spheciform Apoidea have changed since Bohart and
Menke, use this as a practical first-pass sorting key. Confirm genus-level and
species-level identifications with current catalogs and original revisions.

## Before keying

Useful views and characters:

- Hind wing, especially whether the jugal lobe is large and whether it extends
  more than half the length of the anal area behind the claval notch.
- Apex of the mid tibia, especially whether there is one apical spur or two.
- Fore and mid tarsal claws, especially whether they are simple, toothed, or
  cleft.
- Antennal socket position, mandibular socket closure, mandible margin, omaulus
  or mesopleural sulci, scutal carinae, and sternum I ridges.
- Forewing venation, especially number of submarginal cells, stigma size, and
  recurrent-vein position.

Bohart and Menke (1976, pp. 57-61) note that specimens key best when the legs
are somewhat extended and the hind wing is visible. Some characters, especially
claw teeth and plantulae, may require 50-100x magnification.

| 11b | Oblique scutal carina absent; sternum I without a forked basal ridge; ocelli normally developed; labrum usually transverse and wider than long; when three submarginal cells are present, vein 2m-cu enters the second submarginal cell. | Crabronidae (24 California genera) |

Difficult endpoints. The most delicate family separations are Ammoplanidae
versus minute Pemphredonidae, two-spurred Bembicidae versus Astatidae or
Mellinidae, and one-spurred Bembicidae versus Crabronidae. Confirm these with
detailed subfamily or genus keys.

## Sexing spheciform wasps

Use more than one character when possible.

These screens follow Bohart and Menke's morphology discussions of antennae and
the gaster (1976, pp. 9-10, 20-21).

- Visible metasomal terga: females usually show six visible tergites; males
  usually show seven. Bohart and Menke describe exceptions in some groups, so
  treat this as a fast screen rather than a proof.
- Antennae: females usually have 12 antennomeres, made of scape, pedicel, and
  10 flagellomeres. Males usually have 13 antennomeres, made of scape, pedicel,
  and 11 flagellomeres. Some male flagellomeres may be fused or modified.
- Female apex: the last visible tergum may form a pygidial plate, especially
  in fossorial forms. The plate can function as a dirt-pushing surface.
- Male apex: sternum VIII and genital structures are often useful for generic
  or species determinations.
- Legs and antennae: females of digging species often have fore-tarsal rake
  setae; males more often have modified antennae or legs. Absence of a strong
  rake does not by itself prove the specimen is male.

For long-petioled Sphecidae such as `Ammophila`, `Podalonia`, `Sceliphron`,
and related genera, count dorsal tergal plates from the expanded distal part of
the metasoma. Do not count every apparent division along the petiole as a
separate visible tergum.
