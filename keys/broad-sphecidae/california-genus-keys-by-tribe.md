Workflow. Use the companion family key first. Within each family, use the new
intermediate key to determine a tribe, subtribe, or practical morphological
group. Then use only the small genus key for that group.

Taxonomic convention. Current family names are followed by the historical
tribes and subtribes used in Bohart and Menke (1976), because those groupings
correspond directly to the available generic keys. "Practical group" is used
where modern Ammoplanidae lacks a comparably useful formal subdivision for the
California fauna.

Exclude Pompilidae first. A pompilid usually has a curved, mobile pronotum
reaching or nearly reaching the tegula, a transverse or oblique groove across
the mesopleuron, two hind-tibial spurs, and long spinose legs. Pompilidae are
not included in the 90 genera below.

### How to use these keys

- Determine family first, then the intermediate tribe/subtribe/group, and only then the genus.

- Expose the wings, middle-tibial apex, mandibular base, propodeum, first metasomal sternum, and tarsal claws. Many characters require 50-100x magnification.

- Several couplets are sex-specific. When the stated sex is unavailable, use the accompanying nonsexual character or confirm with the cited revision.

- Endpoints shaded pale yellow and marked [confirm] are screening endpoints for especially subtle or recently revised genera.

## Character glossary

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Acetabular carina | Carina on the lower anterior mesopleuron near the midcoxa; continuity with other pleural carinae is important in psenid and crabronine keys. |
| Admedian lines | Paired longitudinal lines or grooves on the anterior half of the mesoscutum. |
| Claval notch | Indentation on the posterior margin of the hind wing used as a reference when estimating jugal-lobe size. |
| Clypeus | Sclerite forming the lower central face immediately above the labrum. |
| Cu-a | Crossvein used as a landmark for determining where vein M diverges in the hind wing. |
| Episternal sulcus | Longitudinal or oblique mesopleural groove usually beginning near the subalar fossa. |
| Frontoclypeal suture | Boundary between frons and clypeus; the distance from the antennal sockets to this suture is diagnostic. |
| Holoptic / dichoptic | Eyes meeting on the vertex / remaining separated on the vertex. |
| Hypersternaulus | Horizontal lower mesopleural carina or groove above the midcoxa. |
| Jugal lobe | Basal posterior lobe of the hind wing. |
| Malar space | Shortest distance between the lower eye margin and mandibular base. |
| Marginal cell | Closed or partly open cell along the leading outer part of the forewing, immediately beyond the stigma. |
| Midtibial spur | Movable apical spur at the end of the middle tibia; count one or two and inspect both sides. |
| Oblique scutal carina | Diagonal ridge extending inward from near the posterior margin of the tegula; common in several bembicid lineages. |
| Omaulus | Vertical or oblique carina on the anterior mesopleuron below the pronotal lobe. |
| Orbital fovea | Small depression or groove along the inner eye margin, often near the vertex. |
| Petiole | Narrow basal part of the metasoma. Its length and whether it is formed by sternum alone or by tergum and sternum are important. |
| Placoids | Flattened sensory areas or plates on male flagellomeres. |
| Plantula | Small pad between tarsomeres; visibility may require high magnification. |
| Prestigmal length | Length of the first submarginal cell before the stigma; especially important in Stizini and Bembicini. |
| Propodeal enclosure | Delimited dorsal basal area of the propodeum, often U-shaped, triangular, or sculptured. |
| Psammophore | Basket or brush of specialized setae used to transport sand. |
| Pygidial plate | Differentiated dorsal apical area of the metasoma, often flat, grooved, or setose. |
| Scapal basin | Depressed facial area surrounding or above the antennal scapes. |
| Scrobal sulcus | Mesopleural groove associated with the scrobe below the wing base. |
| Submarginal cells | Closed cells behind the marginal cell of the forewing; their number and proportions are key characters. |
| Subomaulus | Carina below and approximately parallel to the omaulus. |
| Verticaulus | Vertical mesopleural carina extending upward from the lower pleuron. |
