Working key to 10 current families, with all 90 California genera listed by
family

Scope. This is a California-restricted working key for the spheciform wasps
included in the broad Bohart and Menke concept of Sphecidae. The historical
subfamilies and tribes are cross-walked to 10 current families. The key is
intended for pinned or well-photographed specimens and is not a substitute for
a worldwide family key.

| Current families | California genera | Taxonomic scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 90 | Broad Sphecidae sensu Bohart & Menke |

## How to use the key

- Expose the hind wing, the apex of the mid tibia, and the base of the metasoma whenever possible.

- Begin with the hind-wing jugal lobe and the construction of the first metasomal segment. These are the strongest characters for Sphecidae.

- Use midtibial spur number to divide most of the remaining families. Examine both sides if a spur is hidden.

- For minute wasps, the omaulus, episternal sulcus, stigma, and reduced wing cells may require 50-100x magnification.

- Confirm difficult determinations with a formal key, especially Ammoplanidae versus Pemphredonidae and Bembicidae versus Crabronidae.

Difficult endpoints. The most delicate family separations are Ammoplanidae
versus minute Pemphredonidae, two-spurred Bembicidae versus Astatidae or
Mellinidae, and one-spurred Bembicidae versus Crabronidae. Confirm these with
detailed subfamily or genus keys.

## California genera by current family

Arrangement. Families are presented in approximately the order encountered in
the key. Genera are grouped under their Bohart and Menke subfamily/tribe
placement and alphabetized within each group.

### Sphecidae - 10 genera

Large jugal lobe and the characteristic sternum-I petiole; the traditional
thread-waisted and mud-dauber lineages.

Sphecinae - Ammophilini: Ammophila, Podalonia.

Sphecinae - Sceliphronini: Chalybion, Podium, Sceliphron.

Sphecinae - Sphecini: Chlorion, Isodontia, Palmodes, Prionyx, Sphex.

### Ampulicidae - 1 genus

Cockroach-hunting apoid wasps with two midtibial spurs and toothed or cleft
claws.

Ampulicinae - Dolichurini: Paradolichurus.

### Astatidae - 3 genera

Two-spurred, sessile-metasoma true-bug hunters, usually associated with open
ground.

Astatinae - Astatini: Astata, Diploplectron, Dryudella.

### Mellinidae - 2 genera

A heterogeneous small family represented in California by Mellinus and
Xenosphex, which enter the key through separate character combinations.

Nyssoninae - Mellinini: Mellinus.

Xenosphecinae - Xenosphecini: Xenosphex.

### Psenidae - 5 genera

Mostly petiolate, one-spurred wasps with three submarginal cells and antennal
sockets high on the face.

Pemphredoninae - Psenini: Ammopsen, Mimesa, Mimumesa, Pluto, Psenulus.

### Ammoplanidae - 9 genera

Minute wasps with strongly reduced forewing venation; formerly treated within
Pemphredoninae/Pemphredonini.

Pemphredoninae - Pemphredonini (Ammoplanina): Ammoplanellus, Ammoplanops,
Ammoplanus, Andrewsena, Mohavena, Parammoplanus, Pulverro, Riparena,
Timberlakena.

### Pemphredonidae - 5 genera

Mostly small aphid- and thrips-hunting wasps with reduced venation, but
generally less extreme than Ammoplanidae.

Pemphredoninae - Pemphredonini: Diodontus, Passaloecus, Pemphredon, Spilomena,
Stigmus.

### Philanthidae - 5 genera

Robust bee-, ant-, beetle-, or hymenopteran-hunting wasps with closed
mandibular sockets.

Philanthinae - Philanthini: Philanthus.

Philanthinae - Aphilanthopsini: Aphilanthops, Clypeadon.

Philanthinae - Cercerini: Cerceris, Eucerceris.

### Bembicidae - 26 genera

A morphologically diverse family including nyssonines, gorytines, stizines, and
sand wasps with reduced or recessed ocelli.

Nyssoninae - Alyssonini: Alysson, Didineis.

Nyssoninae - Nyssonini: Epinysson, Foxia, Hyponysson, Metanysson, Nysson,
Zanysson.

Nyssoninae - Gorytini: Arigorytes, Gorytes, Hapalomellinus, Harpactus,
Hoplisoides, Oryttus, Sphecius, Trichogorytes.

Nyssoninae - Stizini: Bembecinus, Stizoides, Stizus.

Nyssoninae - Bembicini: Bembix, Bicyrtes, Microbembex.

Nyssoninae - Bembicini (Stictiellina): Glenostictia, Steniolia, Stictiella,
Xerostictia.

### Crabronidae - 24 genera

The largest California family in this working treatment, including larrines,
miscophines, trypoxylonines, oxybelines, and crabronines.

Larrinae - Larrini: Larropsis, Liris, Prosopigastra, Tachysphex, Tachytes.

Larrinae - Miscophini: Lyroda, Miscophus, Nitela, Plenoculus, Solierella.

Larrinae - Trypoxylonini: Pisonopsis, Trypoxylon.

Crabroninae - Oxybelini: Belomicrus, Oxybelus.

Crabroninae - Crabronini: Crabro, Crossocerus, Ectemnius, Encopognathus,
Enoplolindenius, Lestica, Lindenius, Moniaecera, Rhopalum, Tracheliodes.

## Brief character glossary

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Claval notch | Indentation along the posterior margin of the hind wing near the end of the claval region; used as a landmark for estimating jugal-lobe size. |
| Jugal lobe | Basal posterior lobe of the hind wing. Its relative area is a major character in the first family couplets. |
| Midtibial spur | Movable apical spur at the end of the middle tibia. Count one or two; a hidden second spur may be visible only from the opposite side. |
| Omaulus | A vertical or oblique ridge on the anterior mesopleuron, descending from below the pronotal lobe. |
| Episternal sulcus | A groove on the mesopleuron; its origin and course help separate Ammoplanidae from Pemphredonidae. |
| Petiole | The narrow basal part of the metasoma. In Sphecidae it is formed principally by sternum I; in many other groups both tergum and sternum contribute. |
| Stigma | Thickened, usually darkened area along the leading edge of the forewing. It is conspicuously enlarged in many minute pemphredonine-grade wasps. |
| Submarginal cells | Closed forewing cells immediately behind the marginal cell. Their number and proportions are important throughout the key. |
| Oblique scutal carina | A diagonal ridge extending inward from near the posterior edge of the tegula; characteristic of several bembicid lineages. |
| Prestigmal length | The portion of the first submarginal cell lying before the stigma; exceptionally long in some Bembicidae. |
