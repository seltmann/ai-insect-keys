# Identification table and working key to Dangermond bee genera

Last updated: 2026-08-14

## Scope

This key covers all 47 normalized genera in the Dangermond Preserve bee
checklist: both direct preserve-box genera and broader mainland candidate
genera. It is a regional working key, not a replacement for the full generic
keys in Michener or BeeGenera. It is intended for adult specimens under
magnification; many couplets will not work reliably from field photographs.

Tier definitions follow the checklist:

- `Expected`: direct genus-resolved GBIF records in the tight preserve box.
- `Nearby candidate`: genus-resolved GBIF records in the broader mainland
  context box, but not yet in the tight preserve box.

Abbreviations: `T1`, `T2`, etc. are metasomal terga; `S1`, `S2`, etc. are
metasomal sterna. "Scopa" means pollen-carrying hairs. "Arolia" are the pads
between the claws.

| Megachilidae | `Hoplitis` | Nearby candidate | Minute to moderate osmiine; T1 anterior surface not broadly concave or sharply margined; parapsidal lines often long; color black to metallic; pale bands frequent. | Male S6 with basal hairless translucent flaps in the generic key; T7 and S6 useful for subgenera. |
| Megachilidae | `Megachile` | Expected | Leafcutter/dauber; female ventral scopa present; arolia absent; small to large, usually with pale tergal hair bands; mandibles often modified. | Male often with modified forelegs or terminalia depending on subgenus; arolia absent; route with `Megachile` subgenus/species keys. |
| Megachilidae | `Osmia` | Expected | Small to moderate, usually metallic blue/green; parapsidal lines punctiform or very short; pale tergal bands usually absent or weak. | Male osmiine; parapsidal lines short; body often metallic; terminalia needed for species. |
| Megachilidae | `Protosmia` | Nearby candidate | Minute to small heriadiform bee; little or no pale tergal banding; female clypeus with median apical spatulate horn in key; hind coxa carinate. | Male with six exposed terga and retracted weak T7 in generic key; only expected species is `P. rubifloris`. |
| Megachilidae | `Stelis` | Nearby candidate | Megachilid brood parasite; scopa absent; black or metallic, with or without yellow/white markings; middle tibia with two apical spines. | Parasite male, not sharply pointed like `Coelioxys`; terminalia and host association often needed for species. |
| Megachilidae | `Trachusa` | Nearby candidate | Moderate to rather large anthidiine; scopa present; strong carinae mostly absent except sometimes pronotal lobe; propodeal foveae and basal pits absent. | Male anthidiine; clypeus/paraocular area often pale in some routes; S8 and arolia help in subgeneric placement. |
| Melittidae | `Hesperapis` | Nearby candidate | Minute to large andreniform melittid; usually with tergal hair bands; female scopa on tibia rather than trochanter/femur; metasoma often flattened and soft. | Andreniform male; family confirmation uses absence of facial fovea, one subantennal suture, and melittid mouthpart characters. |

## Working key

### 1. First-pass family and habit screen

1. Labrum long and broadly articulated with clypeus; first two labial palpal
   segments long, flattened, and sheathlike; forewing usually with two
   submarginal cells; female scopa, if present, on metasomal sterna. -> Go to
   `Megachilidae`

1'. Labrum not as above; female scopa, if present, usually on hind legs or
    absent. -> 2

2. Glossa broadly truncate, emarginate, or bilobed; local genera either
   moderately hairy with pale tergal bands or minute, wasp-like, and nearly
   hairless. -> Go to `Colletidae`

2'. Glossa pointed or not visible; not a clear colletid. -> 3

3. Female with facial foveae, usually two subantennal sutures; ground-nesting,
   nonparasitic bees. Males of some groups may require confirmation by
   subantennal sutures and wing characters. -> Go to `Andrenidae`

3'. Facial foveae absent or not useful; usually one subantennal suture, or
    long-tongued apid habitus. -> 4

4. Bright or dull sweat-bee habitus, usually andreniform; one subantennal
   suture; many genera with weakened wing veins, metallic head/thorax, or red
   parasitic metasoma. -> Go to `Halictidae`

4'. Long-tongued bees with anthophoriform, euceriform, bombiform,
    xylocopiform, nomadiform, or epeoliform habitus. -> Go to `Apidae`

5. If an andreniform bee does not fit Halictidae or Andrenidae and the female
   scopa is clearly tibial rather than trochanteral/femoral, compare
   `Hesperapis`. -> Go to `Melittidae`

### Andrenidae

1. Female with large velvety facial foveae; usually medium-sized andreniform
   bee, often with propodeal scopa and hind-trochanter flocculus. Male often
   `Andrena`-like and sometimes pale-faced. -> `Andrena`

1'. Facial foveae not large and velvety; usually panurgine. -> 2

2. Minute to small; marginal cell short and truncate in most species; body
   often extensively yellow, white, red, or metallic. -> `Perdita`

2'. Marginal cell not the short truncate perditine type. -> 3

3. First recurrent vein meeting or nearly meeting first transverse cubital;
   small, slender, black; male often yellow-clypeate and almost hylaeiform. ->
   `Panurginus`

3'. Body more flattened; face often pale-marked; terga often with yellow
    integumental bands or hair bands; male genitalia with enlarged penis
    valves and gonostyli absent or nearly so. -> `Calliopsis`

### Colletidae

1. Moderately hairy andreniform to apiform bee, usually with pale tergal hair
   bands; face tapering below; second recurrent vein arcuate distally. ->
   `Colletes`

1'. Minute to small, slender, nearly hairless and wasp-like; black with limited
    yellow or white markings; female scopa absent and pollen carried
    internally. -> `Hylaeus`

### Melittidae

1. Andreniform bee with tibial scopa, usually tergal hair bands, and often
   flattened soft metasoma; not fitting `Andrena`, `Halictus`,
   `Lasioglossum`, or `Colletes`. -> `Hesperapis`

### Halictidae

1. Body bright metallic green or blue at least on head and mesosoma. -> 2

1'. Body nonmetallic, weakly metallic, blackish, reddish, or dull greenish. ->
    4

2. Moderate to large; head and thorax bright green or blue; male metasoma
   usually with strong transverse yellow bands; posterior surface of propodeum
   enclosed by carina. -> `Agapostemon`

2'. Smaller bright metallic green, blue, or brassy; male without the
    `Agapostemon` yellow-banded metasoma. -> 3

3. Small augochlorine; inner hind tibial spur with rounded serrations; body
   often more than 5 mm. -> `Augochlorella`

3'. Minute to small rophitine, blackish or greenish but dull and minutely
    roughened; antennae low on face; often Hydrophyllaceae-associated. ->
    `Conanthalictus`

4. Scopa absent; broad-headed cleptoparasite, often black with red metasoma;
   terga shiny and without hair bands; propodeum coarsely areolate. ->
   `Sphecodes`

4'. Scopa present, or specimen is a male of a pollen-collecting genus. -> 5

5. Distal forewing veins weakened; American species without apical tergal hair
   bands; basal tergal tomentum or lateral patches sometimes present. ->
   `Lasioglossum`

5'. Distal forewing veins strong, or specimen is a rophitine with low antennal
    sockets. -> 6

6. Metasomal terga with pale apical hair bands in both sexes; nonmetallic to
   weakly metallic; common sweat-bee habitus. -> `Halictus`

6'. Rophitine habitus, antennae low on face; tergal bands absent or variable.
    -> 7

7. Minute to small, often southern California/desert-associated; metasoma
   sometimes red; tergal hair bands absent. -> `Micralictoides`

7'. Minute to moderate; black to metallic bluish or greenish, sometimes red;
    tergal hair bands sometimes present; many species oligolectic. ->
    `Dufourea`

### Apidae

1. Eyes hairy and hind tibial spurs absent; honey bee habitus. -> `Apis`

1'. Eyes not hairy in that combination, or hind tibial spurs present. -> 2

2. Small, shiny, slender small carpenter bee; body sparsely haired; clypeus
   often with pale median mark; tibial scopa present in female. -> `Ceratina`

2'. Body not small, shiny, and slender in this way. -> 3

3. Very hairy bombiform bee; female queens/workers with corbicula; black,
   yellow, red, or white vestiture pattern. -> `Bombus`

3'. Not a bumble bee. -> 4

4. Large to very large robust carpenter bee, often black or metallic; nests in
   wood or stems; not with the oil-collecting hind-leg scopa of `Centris`. ->
   `Xylocopa`

4'. Not a large carpenter bee. -> 5

5. Moderate to very large anthophoriform bee; hind legs of both sexes with
   longer, denser hairs than in `Anthophora`; oil-collecting structures may be
   present on female fore- and mid-basitarsi. -> `Centris`

5'. Smaller, parasitic, or eucerine/anthophorine; not `Centris`. -> 6

6. Scopa absent in female, or specimen has obvious brood-parasite habitus
   (`Nomada`-, `Epeolus`-, or melectine-like). -> 7

6'. Female with scopa, or male evidently from pollen-collecting eucerine or
    anthophorine group. -> 15

7. Slender, wasp-like, usually red/yellow/black; lacks conspicuous appressed
   pale pubescent bands or patches. -> `Nomada`

7'. Body epeoliform or anthophoriform, usually with appressed pale hair patches
    or parasite-like robust habitus. -> 8

8. Small anthophoriform parasite with patterned wings; metasoma variegated by
   short appressed gray or brown hair; male flagellum greatly thickened. ->
   `Zacosmia`

8'. Wings not patterned in that way; male flagellum not greatly thickened. -> 9

9. Anthophoriform or euceriform parasite, not strongly epeoliform. -> 10

9'. Epeoliform parasite, often with strong pale hair bands or patches. -> 12

10. T1 without long hair like mesosoma; middle and hind claws with broad,
    lobelike inner rami; usually patches or broken bands of white hair. ->
    `Brachymelecta`

10'. T1 with long hair like mesosoma; middle and hind claw inner rami pointed,
     more like outer rami. -> `Melecta`

12. Minute to small; metasoma usually red; lacks well-defined areas of short
    pale hair. -> `Oreopasites`

12'. Short pale pubescence forms bands, spots, or areas. -> 13

13. Minute to small; bands or areas of short white pubescence; host signal
    likely `Hesperapis` or `Conanthalictus`. -> `Townsendiella`

13'. Moderate epeoline parasite; compare T5, S6, palpi, and pygidial plate. ->
     14

14. Female T5 pseudopygidial area usually at least half as long as broad;
    female S6 reduced to transverse bar with lateral processes; male pygidial
    plate more parallel-sided and sinuate; maxillary palpi usually
    three-segmented. -> `Triepeolus`

14'. Female T5 pseudopygidial area shorter, usually much less than half as long
     as broad; female S6 with large disc and apical spatulate processes; male
     pygidial plate sides convergent; maxillary palpi usually two-segmented. ->
     `Epeolus`

15. Forewing closed cells largely hairless and wing surface beyond veins
    coarsely papillate; anthophorine route. -> 16

15'. Forewing hairy throughout or specimen is eucerine/emphorine; not the
     anthophorine bare-cell route. -> 18

16. Marginal cell shorter than distance from its apex to wing tip; first
    recurrent vein ending near middle of second submarginal cell. ->
    `Anthophora`

16'. Marginal cell about as long as distance from its apex to wing tip; first
     recurrent vein ending near apex of second submarginal cell; proboscis in
     repose not reaching beyond forecoxae. -> `Habropoda`

18. Hairy anthophoriform or euceriform bee; pale metasomal hair bands common;
    male T7 with two small apical lobes or points. -> `Diadasia`

18'. Eucerine bee in `Melissodes`/`Eucera`/`Xenoglossa` complex. -> 19

19. Tegula narrowed anteriorly, lateral margin straight or slightly concave in
    anterior half; female mandible simple or only weakly notched; male clypeus
    little to moderately protruding. -> `Melissodes`

19'. Tegula not narrowed in this way, or clypeus strongly protuberant. -> 20

20. North American `Eucera (Synhalonia)` habitus; clypeus strongly protuberant;
    male antennae long and black; usually spring-active. -> `Eucera`

20'. Hairy, fast-flying `Xenoglossa` complex; includes squash/gourd bees; local
     GBIF names `Peponapis` and New World `Tetraloniella` should be treated as
     `Xenoglossa` subgroups for this project. -> `Xenoglossa`

### Megachilidae

1. Scopa absent in female, or specimen is a male of an obvious megachilid brood
   parasite. -> 2

1'. Female scopa present on metasomal sterna, or male associated with a
    pollen-collecting megachilid. -> 4

2. Metasoma tapering apically, sharply pointed in female or ending in several
   teeth in male; usually narrow white tergal bands. -> `Coelioxys`

2'. Metasoma not sharply pointed or coelioxiform. -> 3

3. Middle tibia with two apical spines; black or metallic, with or without
   yellow/white metasomal markings; parasite of megachilids. -> `Stelis`

3'. If small and anthidiine-like with scopa absent but not fitting `Stelis`,
    recheck Apidae parasite route and specimen family placement.

4. Arolia absent; female mandible with five or more close-set teeth; broad,
   squarish, yellow-marked wool-carder. -> `Anthidium`

4'. Arolia present or mandible not with the `Anthidium` close-set multi-toothed
    condition. -> 5

5. Arolia absent; female ventral scopa present; leafcutter or dauber bee,
   usually with pale tergal hair bands. -> `Megachile`

5'. Arolia present, or specimen belongs to anthidiine/osmiine route where
    arolia are not decisive. -> 6

6. Small, very robust anthidiine; subantennal suture long and arcuate;
   preoccipital ridge strongly carinate behind vertex; scutellum truncate and
   overhanging metanotum/propodeum. -> `Anthidiellum`

6'. Not with the `Anthidiellum` truncate-scutellum combination. -> 7

7. Anterior margin of scutum abruptly declivous; yellow/white and black
   anthidiine, sometimes red; male T7 usually curled under with lobes. ->
   `Dianthidium`

7'. Anterior margin of scutum not abruptly declivous in this way. -> 8

8. Strong carinae generally absent except sometimes on pronotal lobe;
   propodeal foveae and basal pits absent; moderate to rather large
   anthidiine, resin cells in soil burrows. -> `Trachusa`

8'. Osmiine or megachiline small-to-moderate bee. -> 9

9. Body very slender, shortest distance between tegulae less than or equal to
   length of scutum; male with six exposed sterna. -> `Chelostoma`

9'. Body less slender. -> 10

10. Parapsidal lines punctiform or at most about three times as long as broad;
    body usually metallic. -> `Osmia`

10'. Parapsidal lines linear or other T1/hind-coxa characters decisive. -> 11

11. Anterior surface of T1 concave and delimited by strong carina; male T6
    usually four-toothed. -> `Ashmeadiella`

11'. T1 not with that `Ashmeadiella` combination. -> 12

12. Hind coxa with ventral longitudinal carina; female clypeus with median
    apical spatulate horn in the generic key; minute to small, little pale
    tergal banding. -> `Protosmia`

12'. Hind coxa without that `Protosmia` combination. -> 13

13. Anterior surface of T1 not broadly concave or sharply margined; parapsidal
    lines often more than half as long as tegula; male S6 with basal hairless
    translucent flaps. -> `Hoplitis`

13'. Anterior surface of T1 broad, flat or concave, and sharply margined above;
     parapsidal lines usually shorter; male S6 without basal flaps. ->
     `Atoposmia`

## Notes on difficult separations

- `Epeolus` and `Triepeolus` males can be difficult; use the epeoline key
  characters rather than color pattern alone.
- `Melissodes`, `Eucera`, and `Xenoglossa` require clean views of tegula,
  clypeus, mandible, galea, and sometimes T7 or S6.
- `Lasioglossum` includes subgenera that GBIF may expose as genera
  (`Dialictus`, `Evylaeus`). This project treats them as subgenera unless a
  later taxonomic decision changes the checklist standard.
- `Bombus`, `Ceratina`, `Anthidium`, `Megachile`, `Osmia`, and
  `Lasioglossum` should each get a separate species-level regional key before
  determinations are treated as final.
