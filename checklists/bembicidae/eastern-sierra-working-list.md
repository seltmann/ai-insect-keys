# Eastern Sierra Bembicidae Working List

Last updated: 2026-08-12

## Scope

This checklist is for Bembicidae specimens from the eastern Sierra and adjacent
east-side habitats, especially Mono County, Inyo County, the Owens Valley, Deep
Springs, the White Mountains, and Great Basin or desert-margin sites on the
east side of the Sierra Nevada.

This replaces the earlier coastal Santa Barbara/Dangermond default for this
workspace. Older `Bembix` documents are retained as genus-level references, but
new Bembicidae work should use the broader family folder and should record
eastern-Sierra evidence separately from coastal or Channel Islands evidence.

## Eastern Sierra `Bembix` Priorities

The existing sources make `Bembix` the best-developed genus in this family
folder. For eastern-Sierra specimens, treat these taxa as the first-pass
working set before applying the full California key.

Elevation is useful specimen context, especially for records from Mammoth, Lee
Vining, the White Mountains, Onion Valley, and other high east-side localities,
but it is not a substitute for the structural characters in the key.

The Evans and Matthews North American `Bembix` key is useful for male
terminalia and species-group placement. When checking a male, record sternite VI
separately from the terminal ventral plate: Evans and Matthews refer to the
figured posterior plate as the ultimate sternite and use sternite VIII wording
in the `amoena` group couplet.

### Female ID Feature Table

| Taxon | Eastern Sierra relevance | Female ID features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Bembix amoena` | Strong candidate. Bohart and Horning list many Inyo and Mono localities, including Bishop, Big Pine, Independence, Lone Pine, Owens Valley, Mammoth, Lee Vining, and White Mountains localities. | Clear wings; long scape; ocellar lenses obliterated; labrum smooth; mandible relatively robust; female scutum usually dark; fore-basitarsal rake usually six setae; midtibial spur at least half as long as the mid basitarsus. |
| `Bembix sayi` | Possible but apparently uncommon in California. Bohart and Horning list a female from Lone Pine Creek, Inyo County. | Similar to `B. amoena`; ocellar lenses obliterated; long scape; fore-basitarsal rake usually six setae; midtibial spur shorter and more slender, clearly less than half as long as the mid basitarsus; females often with discal yellow scutal marks and enclosed black tergal spots. |
| `Bembix americana comata` | Strong candidate. Bohart and Horning list Inyo and Mono localities, including Big Pine, Bishop, Deep Springs, Lone Pine, Olancha, Onion Valley, Benton Station, Grant Lake, and Mammoth. Eastern and northern California populations may show tendencies toward `spinolae`, but the checklist keeps them under `comata` unless a source supports otherwise. | Clear wings; long scape; midocellus usually slitlike or scar-like rather than fully obliterated; labrum all pale; abdomen extensively maculate in dorsal view; midtibia not black-marked; fore-basitarsal rake commonly six developed setae, with 5.5, 6.5, and 7.0 count states recorded in population samples. |
| `Bembix occidentalis` | Eastern-Sierra candidate. Bohart and Horning list Inyo records from Bishop, Independence, and Olancha. | Clear wings; long-scaped route in the female key; mandible slender and nearly straight with very small subapical denticle; labrum rather short; frons narrow; ocelli slitlike; notum somewhat flattened; fore-basitarsal rake usually six large setae plus a small seventh; markings highly variable and may enclose black spots. |
| `Bembix stenebdoma` | Eastern-Sierra/desert-side candidate. Bohart and Horning list Inyo records from Big Pine, Deep Springs College, Lone Pine, and Wyman Canyon - White Mountains. | Short scape; partially developed ocellar lenses; labrum simple in profile and shorter than eye height; female last visible tergite punctate rather than longitudinally rugulose; first intersubmarginal vein nearly straight; fore basitarsus with six to seven rake setae. |
| `Bembix gillaspyi` | Eastern-Sierra/desert-side candidate. Bohart and Horning list an Inyo record from Homewood Canyon. | Short scape; partially developed ocellar lenses; labrum dentate or humped near the middle; second submarginal cell acutely prolonged basad; scutum black except laterally; fore-basitarsal rake with six setae and often a weak seventh. |
| `Bembix rugosa` | Eastern-Sierra/desert-side candidate. Bohart and Horning list Inyo records from Antelope Springs and Deep Springs. | Short scape; partially developed ocelli, often narrow and hard to see; labrum longer than eye height; female last visible tergite or pygidial area longitudinally rugose; second submarginal cell acutely prolonged basad; fore-basitarsal rake with seven or eight setae. |
| `Bembix dentilabris` | Eastern-Sierra/desert-side candidate. ### Male ID Feature Table

| Taxon | Eastern Sierra relevance | Male ID features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Bembix amoena` | Strong candidate; broad Inyo and Mono evidence. | Seven visible tergites; sternite VI with an acutely angled backward projection bifurcate at the apex; Evans and Matthews describe the sternite VI median process as flattened beneath and bifid apically, with sternite VIII relatively broad and tapering apically; midtibial spur reaches to or beyond the middle of the mid basitarsus; cuspis relatively broad; midfemur serrate beneath; tergite VII simple; sternite II usually with a low, not hooked keel. |
| `Bembix sayi` | Possible regional species; Bohart and Horning saw only two California females, but males can be keyed structurally if encountered. | Similar to `B. amoena`; sternite VI projection bifurcate at apex; Evans and Matthews keep it in the `amoena` group but separate it by the midtibial spur not reaching the middle of the mid basitarsus and by a slender, tapering cuspis; generally a little smaller than `B. amoena`, but size is secondary. |
| `Bembix americana comata` | Strong candidate; widespread in Inyo and Mono. | Sternite VI with a backward-pointing projection tapering to a sharp or narrowly rounded apex; midfemur conspicuously serrate; clypeus mainly yellow, basal margin rarely black; tergal pale markings almost always whitish; male species characters include sternite II median keel, stout posteriorly directed sternite VI spine, capitate digitus, and fingerlike cuspis. |
| `Bembix occidentalis` | Eastern-Sierra candidate from Inyo records. | Tergite VII not angularly expanded toward base; midocellus usually slitlike and without an obvious lens; sternite VI bituberculate or with two parallel submedian ridges; sternite II with a sharp median carina or small to moderate keel; flagellomere I only a little longer than scape. |
| `Bembix stenebdoma` | Eastern-Sierra/desert-side candidate from Inyo and White Mountains records. | Tergite VII angularly expanded toward base; midocellus with an obvious short lens; sternite VI with a somewhat curved transverse ridge; sternite II normally with a prominent keellike projection; labrum not dentate; mid basitarsus bowed out in front; first intersubmarginal vein nearly straight. |
| `Bembix gillaspyi` | Eastern-Sierra/desert-side candidate from Inyo records. | Tergite VII angularly expanded toward base; midocellus with an obvious short lens; sternite VI with a curved transverse ridge; sternite II with prominent keellike projection; labrum dentate near middle in profile; mid basitarsus not bowed out in front; first intersubmarginal vein markedly sinuate. |
| `Bembix rugosa` | Eastern-Sierra/desert-side candidate from Inyo records. | Tergite VII not angularly expanded toward base but with numerous longitudinal rugulae; labrum longer than eye height; sternite VI with a strong curved transverse ridge; midfemur slightly irregular beneath but not serrate; gonostyle broadly and obliquely truncate toward apex. |
| `Bembix dentilabris` | Eastern-Sierra/desert-side candidate from Inyo records. | Tergite VII angularly expanded toward base; sternites VI and II simple in the relevant key branch; midleg basitarsus with a group of stout bristles toward inner middle; shiny tylus beneath terminal flagellomere extends well beyond middle; midfemur multispinulose; prominent U-shaped discal yellow mark on scutum. |
| `Bembix frommeri` | Special eastern-Inyo male taxon from Deep Springs and Wyman Canyon - White Mountains. | Male only in Bohart and Horning; related to `B. dentilabris`; mostly dark scutal disc; short shiny tylus beneath terminal flagellomere; midfemur only a little irregular beneath, not multispinulose; male fore basitarsus with eight or nine setae; shorter spiracular lobes on tergite VII. |
| `Bembix inyoensis` | Inyo/Death Valley taxon. | Male characters not yet scored in this working repository. Check Kimsey and Kimsey (1981) before using in a key. |

## Immediate Specimen Workflow

1. Confirm that the specimen keys to Bembicidae rather than Crabronidae,
   Mellinidae, or Astatidae.
2. Use `keys/bembicidae/california-genera-key.md` to reach genus.
3. If the specimen is `Bembix`, use
   `keys/bembicidae/california-bembix-foreleg-spines.md` and prioritize the
   eastern-Sierra taxa listed above.
4. Record sex, county, exact locality, elevation, date, forewing condition,
   midtibial spur length, labrum profile, ocellar condition, and female
   fore-basitarsal rake count when available.
