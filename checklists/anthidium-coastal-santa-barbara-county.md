# Checklist: possible Anthidium species from coastal Santa Barbara County, California

Last updated: 2026-07-31

## Scope

This checklist is an initial working list of possible `Anthidium` species for an
insect-identification project focused on coastal Santa Barbara County,
California. "Coastal" is treated as the mainland coastal belt and adjacent low
foothills from the Lompoc/Point Conception area through Goleta, Santa Barbara,
and Montecito. Channel Islands records are kept separate because they may be
important for regional keys but are not mainland coastal records.

This is a candidate list, not a final faunal treatment. It combines
species-level occurrence evidence from GBIF and iNaturalist with taxonomic and
distribution context from `Exotic Bee ID` and Gonzalez and Griswold (2013).
Genus-only `Anthidium` records and DNA/BOLD placeholder names are noted but not
treated as species checklist entries.

A repository copy of Gonzalez and Griswold (2013), the main Western Hemisphere
revision and key, is stored at
[`references/Gonzalez-Griswold-2013-Anthidium-Western-Hemisphere-key.pdf`](../references/Gonzalez-Griswold-2013-Anthidium-Western-Hemisphere-key.pdf).
For this checklist, all 92 species-account distribution statements in that PDF
were scanned for California, cismontane California, coastal, chaparral,
Mediterranean California, southern California, and nearby Baja/desert range
signals.

## Mainland coastal and low-foothill candidates

| Include | Species | Evidence from Santa Barbara County | Notes for the identification project |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core | `Anthidium palliventre` Cresson, 1878 | GBIF has multiple species-level preserved-specimen records from Coal Oil Point Reserve, including dunes and coastal scrub, plus records from Point Conception, Lompoc, and San Miguel Island. iNaturalist also has species-level Santa Barbara County observations. | Strongest coastal candidate. Gonzalez and Griswold (2013) treat this as a coastal dune species from southern California to northern Oregon, 0-200 m, and note that eastern California Owens Valley records are suspect. |
| Core | `Anthidium maculosum` Cresson, 1878 | GBIF has a mainland iNaturalist research-grade record from Montecito and many Channel Islands preserved-specimen records. iNaturalist has a Santa Barbara County species-level observation. | Mainland coastal candidate, but local mainland evidence appears sparse compared with island evidence. Gonzalez and Griswold (2013) treat this as broadly western, including Mediterranean California grassland, chaparral, and woodland, not as a coastal specialist. |
| Core | `Anthidium illustre` Cresson, 1879 | GBIF/iNaturalist include species-level research-grade observations from the Santa Barbara/Goleta low-foothill area. | Include as a likely mainland coastal/foothill candidate. Gonzalez and Griswold (2013) report this from California, southern Oregon, southwestern Utah, New Mexico, and Baja California, and note it is particularly abundant in Mediterranean scrub habitats. |
| Core, verify | `Anthidium tenuiflorae` Cockerell, 1907 | iNaturalist has multiple recent research-grade observations at UCSB. I did not find a matching species-level GBIF Santa Barbara County preserved-specimen record in this pass. | Include, but flag for expert review or specimen confirmation. Gonzalez and Griswold (2013) call it perhaps the most widespread North American `Anthidium`, present in California and Baja California, occupying many non-desert ecoregions. |
| Core, verify | `Anthidium mormonum` Cresson, 1878 | iNaturalist has recent Santa Barbara County species-level observations, including one low-foothill/coastal-area record; GBIF county facet returned a species-level county record. | Regionally plausible; keep as a candidate and verify against similar `Anthidium` species. Gonzalez and Griswold (2013) report it from Pacific Coast states and adjacent regions, largely in forests, Mediterranean California chaparral and woodlands, shrublands, and shrub steppe. |
| Core, verify | `Anthidium utahense` Swenk, 1914 | iNaturalist/GBIF have recent Santa Barbara County species-level observations from the south-coast/foothill area. | Include provisionally; verify carefully against similar small `Anthidium`, especially `A. mormonum`. Gonzalez and Griswold (2013) report it as widespread west of the Rocky Mountains, occupying California chaparral and woodlands, montane and coastal forests, shrub steppe, and grasslands, and most numerous in Mediterranean California. |

## Broader mainland Santa Barbara County candidates

| Include | Species | Evidence from Santa Barbara County | Notes for the identification project |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Broader mainland | `Anthidium edwardsii` Cresson, 1878 | GBIF has numerous preserved-specimen records from Lompoc, Camp Cooke, and Sunset Valley. | Good county-level candidate. Gonzalez and Griswold (2013) report it from the Central Valley, Coast Ranges, and Sierra Nevada foothills of California, mostly below 1000 m. Treat as broader mainland Santa Barbara County unless the project scope includes north-coast/Lompoc-area records. |
| Broader mainland, verify habitat fit | `Anthidium palmarum` Cockerell, 1904 | GBIF has preserved-specimen records from Lompoc. | Include in the broader county pool, but with a habitat warning. Gonzalez and Griswold (2013) report it primarily from Mojave, Sonoran, Baja California, and Chihuahuan desert ecoregions, with extensions into the southern San Joaquin Valley and other xeric regions. |

## Range-overlap watchlist from full PDF scan

These species were not surfaced as species-level Santa Barbara County
occurrence candidates in the GBIF/iNaturalist pass, but Gonzalez and Griswold
(2013) give range or ecoregion statements that overlap California, cismontane
California, coastal forests, chaparral, Mediterranean California, southern
California, or nearby Baja/desert systems. They should be kept in mind when
building keys or reviewing unidentified specimens.

| Species | PDF distribution signal | Current checklist treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Anthidium atrifrons` Cresson, 1878 | Pacific Coast states to Wyoming, Colorado, and Arizona, mostly 1000-3000 m elevation. | Watchlist only; plausible in broader California mountains, but not a low coastal Santa Barbara candidate without records. |
| `Anthidium clypeodentatum` Swenk, 1914 | California and Arizona to the Pacific Northwest, northern Great Plains, Colorado, and northern Baja California; infrequently collected across chaparral, desert-margin, shrub-steppe, montane forest, and grassland ecoregions. | Watchlist only; no Santa Barbara County species-level record found in this pass. |
| `Anthidium dammersi` Cockerell, 1937 | Southern California, Arizona to southwestern New Mexico, Nevada, Utah, southwestern Wyoming, and Baja California; xeric regions including Great Basin, Colorado Plateau, and Mojave Desert. | Watchlist only; possible in broader southern California, but coastal Santa Barbara fit is weak without records. |
| `Anthidium jocosum` Cresson, 1878 | California, southern Nevada, Arizona, southwestern New Mexico, Baja California, and Sonora; mostly desert, but also drier coastal sage and chaparral margins of southern San Joaquin Valley in cismontane California. | Watchlist only; no Santa Barbara County species-level record found in this pass. |
| `Anthidium pallidiclypeum` Jaycox, 1967 | Baja California and California, including South Coast Range, southern California mountains, and mountainous Mojave Desert areas. | Watchlist only; possible if the project expands into interior foothill/mountain sites. |
| `Anthidium placitum` Cresson, 1879 | California, Oregon, southern Washington to western South Dakota, Colorado, Arizona, and northern Baja California; represented in shrub steppe, Colorado Plateau shrublands, California chaparral and woodlands, and forest ecosystems. | Watchlist only; no Santa Barbara County species-level record found in this pass. |

## Full PDF scan exclusions

The full Gonzalez and Griswold (2013) species-account distribution scan also
reviewed California or nearby western taxa that currently appear unlikely for a
coastal Santa Barbara County checklist: `Anthidium atripes`, `A. banningense`,
`A. cockerelli`, `A. cochimi`, `A. emarginatum`, `A. formosum`,
`A. paroselae`, `A. porterae`, `A. quetzalcoatli`, `A. schwarzi`, and
`A. sonorense`. These are treated as exclusions for now because their accounts
emphasize desert, east-of-Sierra/intermountain, montane, or non-cismontane
distributions rather than the mainland coastal Santa Barbara belt.
