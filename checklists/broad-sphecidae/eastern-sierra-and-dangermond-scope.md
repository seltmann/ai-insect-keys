# Working scope: California broad-Sphecidae with Dangermond and Eastern Sierra flags

Last updated: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This note records the current working scope for California spheciform wasps in
the broad `Sphecidae` sense: Apoidea excluding bees. It is meant to support
future checklist and key work for the Dangermond Preserve, coastal Santa
Barbara County, and the Eastern Sierra.

The shared working notes treated broad Sphecidae as a 90-genus California
checklist distributed across 10 current family buckets. The user-supplied DOCX
files are now represented in companion repository pages for the
[family key and 90-genera list](../../keys/broad-sphecidae/california-family-key-and-genera.md)
and the
[small genus keys by tribe](../../keys/broad-sphecidae/california-genus-keys-by-tribe.md).
This document keeps the regional flag definitions and Sierra-priority starting
points that still need occurrence-level auditing.

## Regional flag legend

Use these flags consistently in later genus and species tables:

| Flag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Expected | Strong records or strong ecological fit for the region. |
| Likely | Probably present, but not yet documented locally in the sources checked. |
| Possible | California genus with plausible regional occurrence. |
| Unlikely | California genus, but poor geographic or habitat fit for the region. |

Regional scope:

- Dangermond means the Jack and Laura Dangermond Preserve and adjacent mainland
  coastal Santa Barbara County context. Do not transfer Channel Islands-only
  signals to mainland Dangermond records without specimen or literature
  evidence.
- Eastern Sierra means the eastern Sierra Nevada slope plus adjacent Mono and
  Inyo County valleys, including Owens Valley. Treat desert-floor Inyo records
  separately from montane Sierra records when habitat matters.

## Current family scope

Bohart and Menke source anchors used for this table include their
family-character discussion (1976, pp. 27-28), morphology discussion
(pp. 9-10, 20-21), and subfamily/tribe keys (pp. 57-61).

| Current family | Working California genera in shared checklist | Bohart and Menke route or screen | Regional notes |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Ammoplanidae | 9 | Small one-spurred pemphredonine-like route; enlarged stigma, one recurrent vein, omaulus absent. | Microscope-priority family; not usually a field-photo starting point. |
| Ampulicidae | 1 | Ampulicinae route; two midtibial spurs, toothed or cleft claws, small or absent jugal lobe. | Possible in California scope; verify with genus-level records before regional flagging. |
| Astatidae | 3 | Astatinae route; two midtibial spurs, sessile metasoma, large hind-wing jugal lobe. | Open sandy habitats make Eastern Sierra records plausible. |
| Bembicidae | 26 | Broad bembicine/nyssonine routes, including one- and two-spurred groups. | High-priority group for sandy sites, river margins, washes, dunes, and open flats. |
| Crabronidae | 24 | One-spurred crabronine/larrine-type routes after bembicid, pemphredonid, psenid, and philanthid exclusions. | Diverse group; many genera need separate keys. |
| Mellinidae | 2 | `Mellinus` route plus `Xenosphex` route in the shared family split. | `Xenosphex` is a southwestern watchlist taxon; verify California and regional records. |
| Pemphredonidae | 5 | Small one-spurred pemphredonine route with omaulus or mesopleural sulcus present. | Microscope-priority family; expect twig, stem, or cavity nesters among relevant taxa. |
| Philanthidae | 5 | Philanthine route; closed mandibular socket and high antennal sockets. | Likely in open sandy sites and flower-rich habitats, but genus-level records are needed. |
| Psenidae | 5 | Psenini route; three submarginal cells, high antennal sockets, small stigma. | Likely but needs specimen-level or catalog support for the target regions. |
| Sphecidae | 10 | True sternal petiole plus large hind-wing jugal lobe; includes long-petioled and mud-dauber forms. | Strongly relevant to both Dangermond and the Eastern Sierra. |

## Sierra-priority starting points

These are the first groups to audit for Eastern Sierra or Dangermond keys
because they are already represented in sources reviewed for this repository or
are prominent California broad-Sphecidae groups.

| Genus or group | Current family | Dangermond flag | Eastern Sierra flag | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Bembix` | Bembicidae | Likely | Expected | Existing repository work places `Bembix americana comata` as the strongest mainland Santa Barbara/Dangermond candidate. Bohart and Horning document Sierra and Eastern Sierra relevance through taxa such as `B. amoena`, `B. americana comata`, `B. sayi`, and `B. frommeri`; the current repository checklist separately adds post-1971 `B. inyoensis` from Death Valley/Inyo sources. |
| `Bicyrtes` | Bembicidae | Possible | Likely | Bohart and Horning record California bembicines including `Bicyrtes ventralis` from Sierra-linked localities such as Fresno/Huntington Lake, Tuolumne, and Tulare. `Bicyrtes` prey are often Heteroptera. |
| `Microbembex` | Bembicidae | Possible | Likely | Bohart and Horning records for `Microbembex californica` include interior and Sierra-adjacent localities such as Inyo/Deep Springs, Mono/Convict Lake, Plumas/Vinton, El Dorado/Chile Bar, Fresno/Kennedy Meadow, and Madera/Lake Millerton. |
| `Stictiella` and `Steniolia` | Bembicidae | Possible | Likely | Treat as a Sierra watchlist group. Bohart and Menke (1976, p. 1) mention `Steniolia` aggregations near Truckee; generic and California distribution details need a dedicated source pass. |
| `Ammophila`, `Podalonia`, `Prionyx`, and `Sphex` | Sphecidae | Likely | Likely | These route through the true sternal petiole plus large jugal lobe screen. They should be among the first modern Sphecidae genera checked for both regions. |
| `Chalybion` and `Sceliphron` | Sphecidae | Likely | Possible to likely | Mud-dauber and cavity/mud-nesting routes in Sceliphronini. Good targets for building a practical field-photo screen after family sorting. |
| `Astata`, `Diploplectron`, and `Dryudella` | Astatidae | Possible | Possible to likely | Use the two-spur plus large-jugal-lobe screen and confirm with genus-level records from sandy or open habitats. |
| `Cerceris`, `Philanthus`, `Aphilanthops`, and allies | Philanthidae | Likely | Likely | Flower-visiting and prey-specialist wasps that may be common in open, sandy, or scrub habitats. Use the closed mandibular socket and high-antennal-socket screen. |
| Small pemphredonid, ammoplanid, and psenid genera | Ammoplanidae, Pemphredonidae, Psenidae | Possible | Possible | These are important for a complete checklist but will need microscope characters and careful source work before confident regional flags. |
| `Crabro`, `Crossocerus`, `Ectemnius`, `Oxybelus`, `Trypoxylon`, and allies | Crabronidae | Likely | Likely | Diverse one-spurred groups. Family placement is only a first pass; build genus keys separately from current catalogs and regional records. |
