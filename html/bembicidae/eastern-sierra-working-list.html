Eastern Sierra Bembicidae Working List

Last updated: 2026-08-12

Scope

This checklist is for Bembicidae specimens from the eastern Sierra and adjacent east-side habitats, especially Mono County, Inyo County, the Owens Valley, Deep Springs, the White Mountains, and Great Basin or desert-margin sites on the east side of the Sierra Nevada.

This replaces the earlier coastal Santa Barbara/Dangermond default for this workspace. Older Bembix documents are retained as genus-level references, but new Bembicidae work should use the broader family folder and should record eastern-Sierra evidence separately from coastal or Channel Islands evidence.

Eastern Sierra Bembix Priorities

The existing sources make Bembix the best-developed genus in this family folder. For eastern-Sierra specimens, treat these taxa as the first-pass working set before applying the full California key.

Elevation is useful specimen context, especially for records from Mammoth, Lee Vining, the White Mountains, Onion Valley, and other high east-side localities, but it is not a substitute for the structural characters in the key.

The Evans and Matthews North American Bembix key is useful for male terminalia and species-group placement. When checking a male, record sternite VI separately from the terminal ventral plate: Evans and Matthews refer to the figured posterior plate as the ultimate sternite and use sternite VIII wording in the amoena group couplet.

Male ID Feature Table

TaxonEastern Sierra relevanceMale ID features
Bembix amoenaStrong candidate; broad Inyo and Mono evidence.Seven visible tergites; sternite VI with an acutely angled backward projection bifurcate at the apex; Evans and Matthews describe the sternite VI median process as flattened beneath and bifid apically, with sternite VIII relatively broad and tapering apically; midtibial spur reaches to or beyond the middle of the mid basitarsus; cuspis relatively broad; midfemur serrate beneath; tergite VII simple; sternite II usually with a low, not hooked keel.
Bembix sayiPossible regional species; Bohart and Horning saw only two California females, but males can be keyed structurally if encountered.Similar to B. amoena; sternite VI projection bifurcate at apex; Evans and Matthews keep it in the amoena group but separate it by the midtibial spur not reaching the middle of the mid basitarsus and by a slender, tapering cuspis; generally a little smaller than B. amoena, but size is secondary.
Bembix americana comataStrong candidate; widespread in Inyo and Mono.Sternite VI with a backward-pointing projection tapering to a sharp or narrowly rounded apex; midfemur conspicuously serrate; clypeus mainly yellow, basal margin rarely black; tergal pale markings almost always whitish; male species characters include sternite II median keel, stout posteriorly directed sternite VI spine, capitate digitus, and fingerlike cuspis.
Bembix occidentalisEastern-Sierra candidate from Inyo records.Tergite VII not angularly expanded toward base; midocellus usually slitlike and without an obvious lens; sternite VI bituberculate or with two parallel submedian ridges; sternite II with a sharp median carina or small to moderate keel; flagellomere I only a little longer than scape.
Bembix stenebdomaEastern-Sierra/desert-side candidate from Inyo and White Mountains records.Tergite VII angularly expanded toward base; midocellus with an obvious short lens; sternite VI with a somewhat curved transverse ridge; sternite II normally with a prominent keellike projection; labrum not dentate; mid basitarsus bowed out in front; first intersubmarginal vein nearly straight.
Bembix gillaspyiEastern-Sierra/desert-side candidate from Inyo records.Tergite VII angularly expanded toward base; midocellus with an obvious short lens; sternite VI with a curved transverse ridge; sternite II with prominent keellike projection; labrum dentate near middle in profile; mid basitarsus not bowed out in front; first intersubmarginal vein markedly sinuate.
Bembix rugosaEastern-Sierra/desert-side candidate from Inyo records.Tergite VII not angularly expanded toward base but with numerous longitudinal rugulae; labrum longer than eye height; sternite VI with a strong curved transverse ridge; midfemur slightly irregular beneath but not serrate; gonostyle broadly and obliquely truncate toward apex.
Bembix dentilabrisEastern-Sierra/desert-side candidate from Inyo records.Tergite VII angularly expanded toward base; sternites VI and II simple in the relevant key branch; midleg basitarsus with a group of stout bristles toward inner middle; shiny tylus beneath terminal flagellomere extends well beyond middle; midfemur multispinulose; prominent U-shaped discal yellow mark on scutum.
Bembix frommeriSpecial eastern-Inyo male taxon from Deep Springs and Wyman Canyon - White Mountains.Male only in Bohart and Horning; related to B. dentilabris; mostly dark scutal disc; short shiny tylus beneath terminal flagellomere; midfemur only a little irregular beneath, not multispinulose; male fore basitarsus with eight or nine setae; shorter spiracular lobes on tergite VII.

Immediate Specimen Workflow

  1. Confirm that the specimen keys to Bembicidae rather than Crabronidae, Mellinidae, or Astatidae.
  2. Use keys/bembicidae/california-genera-key.md to reach genus.
  3. If the specimen is Bembix, use keys/bembicidae/california-bembix-foreleg-spines.md and prioritize the eastern-Sierra taxa listed above.
  4. Record sex, county, exact locality, elevation, date, forewing condition, midtibial spur length, labrum profile, ocellar condition, and female fore-basitarsal rake count when available.
