Eastern Sierra Tiphiidae Genus Key

Last updated: 2026-08-27

Scope

This is a working genus-level key for tiphiid and tiphiid-like wasps from the same strict Eastern Sierra scope used in the Vespidae pages: Inyo plus Mono County, California.

Taxonomy note: this page follows the occurrence screen pragmatically. GBIF currently routes Myzinum, Methocha, Pterombrus, and related former tiphiids under family Tiphiidae, while other current resources often separate some of those lineages as Thynnidae. For the local key, Myzinum is kept as a keyed broad-tiphiid endpoint because strict Mono County records occur under that route.

Use this as a specimen key, not a photo shortcut. Useful characters include winged versus wingless sex, ocellus size, forewing cubital/discoidal cells, the first cubital cell, sternum I and sternum II carinae, the pronotal form, and color pattern.

Fast Route

1a. Large, slender flower wasp, often black with conspicuous yellow bands or spots on the metasoma; male with long straight antennae and a conspicuous upcurved apical abdominal hook or pseudostinger; female more robust, often with shorter or curled antennae. Myzinum - broad/former Tiphiidae; current family placement may be Thynnidae in some resources.

1b. Not the Myzinum habitus above; body usually black, dark brown, or small and nocturnal, or with reduced/abbreviated venation. Go to 2.

2a. Brachycistidine form. Male winged, usually nocturnal; ocelli generally enlarged; pronotum short, usually lacking a real dorsal surface; forewing with abbreviated marginal and submedial cells. Female wingless and ant-like, lacking ocelli; generic placement of females is difficult without associated material. Go to the Brachycistidinae route.

2b. Not brachycistidine. Specimen fully winged in both sexes expected; compact tiphiine form, usually black or dark, with normal tiphiine venation. Go to the Tiphiinae route.

Brachycistidinae Route

Use this route mainly for males. Kimsey and Wasbauer (2006) emphasize that brachycistidine taxonomy is based mostly on males because females are wingless, ant-like, nocturnal, and difficult to associate.

B1a. Sternum II with a medial longitudinal ridge, keel, or fold extending posteriorly from the base; scrobal sulcus present; forewing R vein extending along the costal margin for more than two-thirds the length of the stigma. Acanthetropis

B1b. Sternum II without a medial ridge or keel; scrobal sulcus absent. Go to B2.

B2a. Sternum I with a short medial longitudinal carina extending posteriorly from the base; forewing tCu-1 arising at or before the basal third of the first submarginal cell; male digitus short and broadly rounded if genitalia are examined. Colocistis

B2b. Sternum I smooth subbasally, without the medial carina; forewing tCu-1 arising beyond the basal third of the first submarginal cell; male digitus elongate and acute if genitalia are examined. Brachycistis - guardrail; no strict Inyo/Mono GBIF endpoint in this draft.

B2c. Specimen does not match either branch above. Use the full Brachycistidinae key before forcing a local endpoint.

Tiphiinae Route

This route follows the practical BugGuide/Allen-style separation of North American tiphiine genera.

T1a. Forewing first cubital cell bisected by an extra vein. Go to T2.

T1b. Forewing first cubital cell not bisected by an extra vein. Go to T3.

T2a. Body black, without conspicuous pale spots; wings hyaline, translucent brownish, or smoky, but not dark and opaque. Paratiphia

T2b. Body with conspicuous pale spots; wings dark or opaque. Epomidiopteron - guardrail; outside the strict working fauna.

T3a. Terga II-IV without ribbon-like apical fasciae; median area of sternum VI broad and impunctate. Tiphia - guardrail; no strict Inyo/Mono GBIF endpoint in this draft.

T3b. Terga II-IV with ribbon-like apical fasciae, or sternum VI not matching the broad impunctate Tiphia condition. Other tiphiine guardrail; outside this strict working key.

Practical Notes

If the specimen is a wingless ant-like female, first rule out Mutillidae and other wingless aculeates. In brachycistidines the female has a single midtibial spur and lacks ocelli, but the genus-level literature used here is primarily male-based.

If the specimen is large, yellow-banded, and moving around flowers, start with Myzinum. If it is small, black, nocturnal, and came to a light trap, start with Brachycistidinae. If it is compact, black, and fully winged, check the Tiphiinae route and especially the first cubital cell.

If an occurrence label or database name says only Tiphiidae or a BOLD bin, ignore that as an identification endpoint. Use venation and sternal characters first.
