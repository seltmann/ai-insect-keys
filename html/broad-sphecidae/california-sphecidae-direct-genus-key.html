Direct Key to the California Genera of Sphecidae

Last updated: 2026-08-10

Scope. This is a direct genus key for California specimens already determined as Sphecidae in the restricted modern sense. It covers the 10 California genera treated in the broad-Sphecidae working pages:

Ammophila, Chalybion, Chlorion, Isodontia, Palmodes, Podalonia, Podium, Prionyx, Sceliphron, and Sphex.

This version avoids starting from higher-level family or tribe couplets. It assumes the specimen is already sorted to restricted Sphecidae and uses direct generic characters from Bohart and Menke's Sphecinae genus keys and generic descriptions.

Fast Genus Checks

AmmophilaSternum I does not reach sternum II; tergum-I spiracle at or beyond the sternum-I apex.
PodaloniaSternum I reaches or overlaps sternum II; tergum-I spiracle before the sternum-I apex.
SceliphronU-shaped propodeal enclosure, both recurrent veins received by submarginal cell II, no spiracular groove.
ChalybionHind-wing third anal vein long and separated from the margin; episternal sulcus long.
PodiumHind-wing third anal vein short and contiguous with the margin; episternal sulcus short.
ChlorionU-shaped propodeal enclosure plus second recurrent vein received by cell III or interstitial; female clypeus usually five-toothed.
PrionyxPropodeal spiracular groove absent; basal veinlet of submarginal cell II longer than anterior veinlet; inner eye margins straight or broadly bowed inward; inner hind-tibial spur usually coarse and widely spaced; hindtarsal claw with two to five inner teeth; male placoids present in about half of species.
PalmodesFemale clypeal median lobe truncate or shallowly concave with lateral notches; males lack flagellar placoids.
SphexComplete propodeal spiracular groove; petiole shorter than hindtarsomeres II-IV combined; basal veinlet of submarginal cell II equal to or shorter than anterior veinlet; inner eye margins straight below and curving inward near ocelli; inner hind-tibial spur usually finely and closely pectinate; male antenna usually with one or more placoids.
IsodontiaIncomplete or absent propodeal spiracular groove, longer petiole, and oblique claw teeth.

Notes For Difficult Specimens

  • This direct key does not repeat the Sphecinae tribe key. If a specimen fails several couplets, return to the full Bohart and Menke keys before forcing an endpoint.
  • The Podium / Chlorion separation should be confirmed structurally. Color helps, but the hind-wing third anal vein, episternal sulcus, clypeal teeth, and antennal socket position are more important.
  • For males, antennal characters may matter at later species-level steps even when the genus is clear.
  • Male placoids are important in Sphex, but they are not exclusive to Sphex. Bohart and Menke also describe male placoids in Isodontia and in about half of Prionyx; use them with the propodeal groove, petiole, venation, and claw characters.
  • The final Palmodes / Isodontia path includes sex-specific characters. When only a male is available, flagellar placoids and venation become especially important.
