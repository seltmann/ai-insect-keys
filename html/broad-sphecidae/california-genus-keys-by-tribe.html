Workflow. Use the companion family key first. Within each family, use the new intermediate key to determine a tribe, subtribe, or practical morphological group. Then use only the small genus key for that group.

Taxonomic convention. Current family names are followed by the historical tribes and subtribes used in Bohart and Menke (1976), because those groupings correspond directly to the available generic keys. "Practical group" is used where modern Ammoplanidae lacks a comparably useful formal subdivision for the California fauna.

Exclude Pompilidae first. A pompilid usually has a curved, mobile pronotum reaching or nearly reaching the tegula, a transverse or oblique groove across the mesopleuron, two hind-tibial spurs, and long spinose legs. Pompilidae are not included in the 90 genera below.

How to use these keys

  • Determine family first, then the intermediate tribe/subtribe/group, and only then the genus.
  • Expose the wings, middle-tibial apex, mandibular base, propodeum, first metasomal sternum, and tarsal claws. Many characters require 50-100x magnification.
  • Several couplets are sex-specific. When the stated sex is unavailable, use the accompanying nonsexual character or confirm with the cited revision.
  • Endpoints shaded pale yellow and marked [confirm] are screening endpoints for especially subtle or recently revised genera.

Character glossary

TermDefinition
Acetabular carinaCarina on the lower anterior mesopleuron near the midcoxa; continuity with other pleural carinae is important in psenid and crabronine keys.
Admedian linesPaired longitudinal lines or grooves on the anterior half of the mesoscutum.
Claval notchIndentation on the posterior margin of the hind wing used as a reference when estimating jugal-lobe size.
ClypeusSclerite forming the lower central face immediately above the labrum.
Cu-aCrossvein used as a landmark for determining where vein M diverges in the hind wing.
Episternal sulcusLongitudinal or oblique mesopleural groove usually beginning near the subalar fossa.
Frontoclypeal sutureBoundary between frons and clypeus; the distance from the antennal sockets to this suture is diagnostic.
Holoptic / dichopticEyes meeting on the vertex / remaining separated on the vertex.
HypersternaulusHorizontal lower mesopleural carina or groove above the midcoxa.
Jugal lobeBasal posterior lobe of the hind wing.
Malar spaceShortest distance between the lower eye margin and mandibular base.
Marginal cellClosed or partly open cell along the leading outer part of the forewing, immediately beyond the stigma.
Midtibial spurMovable apical spur at the end of the middle tibia; count one or two and inspect both sides.
Oblique scutal carinaDiagonal ridge extending inward from near the posterior margin of the tegula; common in several bembicid lineages.
OmaulusVertical or oblique carina on the anterior mesopleuron below the pronotal lobe.
Orbital foveaSmall depression or groove along the inner eye margin, often near the vertex.
PetioleNarrow basal part of the metasoma. Its length and whether it is formed by sternum alone or by tergum and sternum are important.
PlacoidsFlattened sensory areas or plates on male flagellomeres.
PlantulaSmall pad between tarsomeres; visibility may require high magnification.
Prestigmal lengthLength of the first submarginal cell before the stigma; especially important in Stizini and Bembicini.
Propodeal enclosureDelimited dorsal basal area of the propodeum, often U-shaped, triangular, or sculptured.
PsammophoreBasket or brush of specialized setae used to transport sand.
Pygidial plateDifferentiated dorsal apical area of the metasoma, often flat, grooved, or setose.
Scapal basinDepressed facial area surrounding or above the antennal scapes.
Scrobal sulcusMesopleural groove associated with the scrobe below the wing base.
Submarginal cellsClosed cells behind the marginal cell of the forewing; their number and proportions are key characters.
SubomaulusCarina below and approximately parallel to the omaulus.
VerticaulusVertical mesopleural carina extending upward from the lower pleuron.
