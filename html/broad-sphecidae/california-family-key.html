California broad-Sphecidae family key and field-screen notes

Last updated: 2026-08-10

Scope

This is a working key for California spheciform wasps in the broad Sphecidae sense used by Bohart and Menke (1976): Apoidea excluding bees. It is intended for repository work on Dangermond Preserve and Eastern Sierra specimens, not as a replacement for genus-level revisions.

The key preserves older Bohart and Menke characters but routes specimens to the current family buckets used in the shared working notes:

  • Ammoplanidae
  • Ampulicidae
  • Astatidae
  • Bembicidae
  • Crabronidae
  • Mellinidae
  • Pemphredonidae
  • Philanthidae
  • Psenidae
  • Sphecidae, restricted modern sense

Because the family limits of spheciform Apoidea have changed since Bohart and Menke, use this as a practical first-pass sorting key. Confirm genus-level and species-level identifications with current catalogs and original revisions.

Before keying

Useful views and characters:

  • Hind wing, especially whether the jugal lobe is large and whether it extends more than half the length of the anal area behind the claval notch.
  • Apex of the mid tibia, especially whether there is one apical spur or two.
  • Fore and mid tarsal claws, especially whether they are simple, toothed, or cleft.
  • Antennal socket position, mandibular socket closure, mandible margin, omaulus or mesopleural sulci, scutal carinae, and sternum I ridges.
  • Forewing venation, especially number of submarginal cells, stigma size, and recurrent-vein position.

Bohart and Menke (1976, pp. 57-61) note that specimens key best when the legs are somewhat extended and the hind wing is visible. Some characters, especially claw teeth and plantulae, may require 50-100x magnification.

Fast screen against similar wasps

This screen follows the family-character discussion in Bohart and Menke (1976, pp. 27-28).

  1. Pronotum broadly reaches or touches the tegula, forewings often folded lengthwise at rest, eyes usually deeply emarginate. -> Vespidae, not broad Sphecidae.

1'. Pronotal lobe separated from the tegula; forewings not folded by the vespid mechanism. -> 2

  1. Pronotum mobile over the scutum, posterior margin concave or obtusely angled, pronotal lobe close to or touching the tegula; metatibia with two apical spurs. -> Pompilidae, not broad Sphecidae.

2'. Pronotum essentially fixed to the mesothorax, posterior margin forming a nearly transverse collar and usually separated from the scutum by a constriction; pronotal lobe well separated from the tegula. -> Broad Sphecidae / spheciform Apoidea.

California broad-Sphecidae family key

This key adapts the family-level structure from the shared Sphecidae notes and the diagnostic characters in Bohart and Menke (1976, especially pp. 57-61). Counts in parentheses are working California genus counts from the shared checklist notes, not audited species counts.

  1. First metasomal segment forming a true sternal petiole: the tubular basal segment is sternum I, with tergum I displaced to the distal end; hind wing with large jugal lobe and anal vein. -> Sphecidae, restricted modern sense (10 California genera)

1'. Metasoma sessile, subsessile, or with a petiole made from tergal plus sternal elements; hind-wing jugal lobe absent, small, or not combined with a true sternal petiole. -> 2

  1. Mid tibia with two apical spurs; tarsal claws toothed or cleft; hind-wing jugal lobe small or absent. -> 3

2'. Mid tibia with one apical spur. -> 7

  1. Small southwestern wasp with mandible distinctly notched, toothed, or stepped on the outer-ventral margin. -> Mellinidae, Xenosphex route

3'. Mandible without that outer-ventral notch or step. -> 4

  1. Hind-wing jugal lobe longer than half the anal area; hind-wing M before cu-a; metasoma sessile; forewing usually with three submarginal cells. -> Astatidae (3 California genera)

4'. Hind-wing jugal lobe shorter than half the anal area or otherwise not as above. -> 5

  1. Metasoma distinctly pedunculate; hind-wing M before cu-a; forewing with three submarginal cells; slender fly hunter, often with yellow markings. -> Mellinidae, Mellinus route

5'. Metasoma not as above, or combination of characters different. -> 6

  1. Two-spurred bembicid routes, principally Alyssonini, Nyssonini, and portions of Gorytini and related groups. -> Bembicidae, part (26 California genera total)
  1. Forewing with three submarginal cells; distinct petiole principally sternum I; antennal sockets high on face; stigma smaller than first discal cell. -> Psenidae (5 California genera)

7'. Character combination otherwise. -> 8

  1. Forewing with one or two submarginal cells; stigma enlarged, near or exceeding first discal cell area; one recurrent vein; usually small to minute. -> 9

8'. Forewing, stigma, and recurrent-vein combination otherwise. -> 10

  1. Omaulus absent; no sulcus beneath posterior pronotal lobe; episternal sulcus arising from subalar fossa; one recurrent vein and one or two discoidal cells; usually about 2-5 mm. -> Ammoplanidae (9 California genera)

9'. Omaulus present or mesopleural sulcus descends from beneath posterior pronotal lobe; sometimes with three discoidal cells or a smaller stigma. -> Pemphredonidae (5 California genera)

  1. Mandibular socket closed by extension of the hypostoma; antennal sockets above the clypeal margin; forewing usually with three submarginal cells; body robust to moderate. -> Philanthidae (5 California genera)

10'. Mandibular socket not closed in that way, or character combination otherwise. -> 11

  1. Oblique scutal carina present, or sternum I with paired basal ridges or a forked median ridge, or ocelli reduced/distorted/recessed, or labrum exposed, or prestigmal portion of first submarginal cell exceptionally long. -> Bembicidae (26 California genera total)

11'. Oblique scutal carina absent; sternum I without forked basal ridge; ocelli normal; labrum transverse and wider than long; if three submarginal cells are present, 2m-cu enters the second submarginal cell. -> Crabronidae (24 California genera)

Sexing spheciform wasps

Use more than one character when possible.

These screens follow Bohart and Menke's morphology discussions of antennae and the gaster (1976, pp. 9-10, 20-21).

  • Visible metasomal terga: females usually show six visible tergites; males usually show seven. Bohart and Menke describe exceptions in some groups, so treat this as a fast screen rather than a proof.
  • Antennae: females usually have 12 antennomeres, made of scape, pedicel, and 10 flagellomeres. Males usually have 13 antennomeres, made of scape, pedicel, and 11 flagellomeres. Some male flagellomeres may be fused or modified.
  • Female apex: the last visible tergum may form a pygidial plate, especially in fossorial forms. The plate can function as a dirt-pushing surface.
  • Male apex: sternum VIII and genital structures are often useful for generic or species determinations.
  • Legs and antennae: females of digging species often have fore-tarsal rake setae; males more often have modified antennae or legs. Absence of a strong rake does not by itself prove the specimen is male.

For long-petioled Sphecidae such as Ammophila, Podalonia, Sceliphron, and related genera, count dorsal tergal plates from the expanded distal part of the metasoma. Do not count every apparent division along the petiole as a separate visible tergum.
